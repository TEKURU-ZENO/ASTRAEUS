## 1. Executive Summary & Vision

ASTRAEUS is an autonomous computational universe and scientific intelligence ecosystem designed to bridge observational astronomy, theoretical astrophysics, computational physics, and artificial intelligence. Rather than attempting to train a single monolithic black-box language model, ASTRAEUS constitutes a modular, physics-aware, and epistemically stratified ecosystem of interoperable scientific models, agents, simulators, and data fabrics.

The central ambition of ASTRAEUS is to learn from astronomical observations across heterogeneous physical modalities, model physical systems through differentiable digital twins, discover unexplained astrophysical anomalies, reason over verified scientific literature, formulate testable hypotheses, and autonomously design computational and observational experiments.

## 2. The Core Epistemic Stratification Principle

A foundational design failure of standard AI applications in science is **epistemic conflation**—the failure to formally demarcate empirical measurements from statistical inferences, physical predictions, numerical simulations, and unverified hypotheses.

ASTRAEUS formally enforces a five-tier ontological boundary across all data schemas, inference heads, agent communication protocols, and generated scientific reports:

$$\mathcal{E} \in \{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}},\, \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inf}},\, \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pred}},\, \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sim}},\, \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{hyp}}\}$$

1. **Directly Observed ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}}$):**
   - Calibrated physical detector measurements traceable to certified instruments.
   - Astrometric coordinates (ICRS), calibrated flux densities across defined passbands, photon arrival timestamps (MJD/BJD), and raw gravitational-wave strain time series.
   - Traceability standard: IVOA Provenance Data Model v1.0 and W3C PROV-DM entity-activity links.
2. **Statistically Inferred ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{inf}}$):**
   - Empirical latent parameters or class probabilities derived via machine learning or Bayesian inference, conditioned on explicit prior distributions and likelihood models.
   - Examples: photometric redshifts ($z_{\mathrm{phot}}$), stellar surface gravities ($\log g$), cluster membership probabilities.
   - Requirement: Conformal prediction coverage intervals ($1-\alpha$) and out-of-distribution (OOD) distance metrics.
3. **Physically Predicted ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{pred}}$):**
   - Analytical deductions derived from closed-form physical theories, conservation laws, or analytical equations of state.
   - Examples: relativistic orbital decay rates, hydrostatic stellar profiles, linear perturbation growth factors.
   - Requirement: Dimensional homogeneity, mathematical consistency proofs, and symmetry group verification.
4. **Numerically Simulated ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sim}}$):**
   - Discrete field states generated by forward numerical integration of differential equations (e.g., cosmological N-body simulations, relativistic MHD shock structures).
   - Requirement: Spatial/temporal convergence checks, energy conservation audits, and subgrid recipe registries.
5. **Hypothesized ($\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{hyp}}$):**
   - Unverified physical mechanisms, candidate anomalies, discovered symbolic equations, or proposed cross-messenger event associations awaiting empirical falsification.
   - Requirement: Explicit falsification conditions, multi-agent adversarial challenge clearance, and hypothesis revision trees.

## 4. Architectural Non-Goals: What ASTRAEUS Is Not

- **Not an unconstrained astronomy chatbot:** Will not output ungrounded natural language claims without verifiable provenance.
- **Not a black-box discovery engine:** Never presents an empirical conclusion without full epistemic tier tags and audit trails.
- **Not a brittle monolith:** Decomposed into independent, loosely coupled, open-source Python packages.
- **Not a universal simulation of all atoms:** Relies on validated validity envelopes for each physical simulator.

## 5. Ten Research Laboratories

The ASTRAEUS scientific core is organized into 10 specialized research laboratories:

1. **01 / Cosmic Perception:** Astronomical foundation models and cross-survey representation.
2. **02 / Stellar & Planetary:** Stellar evolution, exoplanet transit detection, atmospheric retrieval.
3. **03 / Galaxy Evolution:** Galaxy morphology, merger trees, star formation histories.
4. **04 / Extreme Physics:** Relativistic compact binaries, black holes, accretion dynamics.
5. **05 / Cosmology:** Large-scale structure, dark matter/energy inference, weak lensing.
6. **06 / Multi-Messenger:** Gravitational-wave, optical, and neutrino event association.
7. **07 / Scientific Discovery:** Open-set anomaly detection and symbolic regression.
8. **08 / Scientific Knowledge:** Epistemic Graph RAG, citation verifiers, literature brain.
9. **09 / Autonomous Observatory:** Constrained RL telescope scheduling, active learning.
10. **10 / Computational Universe:** Differentiable digital twins and counterfactual physics labs.

---

## 6. Three Anchor Research Tracks

While the architecture spans ten laboratories, implementation is staged across three phased anchor tracks:

- **Track A — Autonomous Transient Discovery:** Start first. Multimodal learning, open-world anomaly discovery, evidence Graph RAG, and automated transient investigation.
- **Track B — Computational Universe:** Differentiable cosmological simulations (pmwd), neural operators (FNO/PINO), and field-level Bayesian parameter inference.
- **Track C — Autonomous Experimental Science:** Multi-messenger event association, constrained RL telescope scheduling, and active experimental design.
